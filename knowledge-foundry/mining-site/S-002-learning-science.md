# S-002 — Learning Science Evidence

Observed: 2026-10-07  
Status: **INSPECTED / bounded**  
Role: evidence for instructional-strategy candidates, not universal teaching laws.

## Sources inspected

### Retrieval practice

Agarwal, Nunes & Blunt (2021), *Retrieval Practice Consistently Benefits Student Learning: a Systematic Review of Applied Research in Schools and Classrooms*.

- systematic review;
- 50 classroom experiments;
- 49 effect sizes;
- total n = 5,374;
- majority of effects were medium/large;
- broad settings/content, but only 6% of experiments were in non-WEIRD countries.

Identity: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09595-9

### Worked examples in programming

Muldner, Jennings & Chiarelli (2023), *A Review of Worked Examples in Programming Activities*.

The review distinguishes code-tracing and code-generation examples and reports useful evidence around program visualizations, subgoals and incomplete examples/Parsons puzzles while also identifying research gaps.

Identity: https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1381113

### Worked examples meta-analysis

Barbieri et al. (2023), *A Meta-Analysis of the Worked Examples Effect on Mathematics Performance*.

- 43 articles;
- 55 studies;
- 181 effect sizes;
- average effect g ≈ 0.48;
- substantial context/moderation questions remain.

Identity: https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1364058

### Spacing

Maye & Hurley (2026), *The Effectiveness of Spaced Repetition in Medical Education: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis*.

- 14 studies in review;
- 13 in meta-analysis;
- 21,415 learners;
- positive overall effect on objective tests;
- specifically medical education.

Identity: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41601436/

Sezgin & Bektas (2026), meta-analysis of spaced learning in health-professions education.

- 9 randomized controlled trials;
- positive effects reported for acquisition and retention;
- authors report high overall risk of bias and call for stronger studies.

Identity: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42468294/

### Formative assessment

Karaman (2021), meta-analysis of formative-assessment practices.

- 32 studies / 47 effects;
- positive overall effect;
- evidence base in Turkey, so external generalization is bounded.

Identity: https://doi.org/10.21449/ijate.870300

## What this source can support

- retrieval practice is a strong candidate mechanism for durable learning;
- worked examples deserve explicit consideration, particularly for novices/technical instruction;
- spacing is promising but must preserve domain/evidence limitations;
- formative feedback/assessment can improve learning and should be treated as part of instruction, not merely scoring.

## What it cannot support

- one fixed spacing interval;
- mandatory quizzes in every lesson;
- worked examples as universally superior for all expertise levels/tasks;
- one global effect size transferable to our audience;
- a claim that these mechanisms automatically produce commercial course quality.
